Filing a liability claim in Florida

Homeowners and renters policies often include liability coverage to protect you in the event of a third-party injury or lawsuit. For example, it may apply if a guest is hurt on your property or someone claims you damaged their belongings.

Safety first: if anyone is hurt, call 911 and make sure they get help.

Step-by-step

  1. Document what happened

    Write down the date, time, and location, and what happened in your own words. Take photos of the area and get the names and contact information of anyone involved and any witnesses.

  2. Be careful what you say

    Be polite, but avoid admitting fault or promising to pay for anything. Let your insurer handle those conversations.

  3. Notify your insurer

    Report the incident as soon as possible, even if no one has made a claim against you yet, and write down your claim number. Find your insurance company's claims contact information.

  4. Send legal papers to your insurer right away

    If you receive a demand letter, a summons, or any other legal papers, send a copy to your insurer immediately. These documents can have short response deadlines.

  5. Cooperate with the adjuster's investigation

    An adjuster will review the incident and may ask for statements or documents. Cooperating with the investigation is a requirement under your policy. If you have questions the adjuster cannot answer, reach out to your agent to clarify.

  6. Review the decision

    Your insurer will explain whether the claim is covered. If you disagree with the decision, ask for the explanation in writing and reach out to your agent for help.
